Status: Client details confirmed, ready for implementation

## 1. Page purpose

The homepage needs to help a local customer answer four questions quickly:

1. Does Advanced Carpets provide the service I need?
2. Do they work in my area?
3. Can I trust them with my home or property?
4. What should I do next?

The page should speak equally to homeowners and businesses across Central Otago, the Southern Lakes and Maniototo.

### Actions

- Primary action: **Make an enquiry**
- Secondary action: **View services**
- Urgent water damage action: **Call 021 333 354**

Enquiry-led wording must be used across the page. Buttons should describe the action clearly.

### Non-negotiable enquiry wording rule

Nothing on the public website should ever offer, invite or promise a quote.

Do not use `get a quote`, `request a quote`, `free quote`, `instant quote`, `quote form`, `quotes` or `quoting`. This applies even when the wording appears in source material supplied by the client.

This rule covers every public-facing part of the site:

- Navigation and buttons
- Headings and body copy
- Service cards and FAQs
- Contact forms, field labels and confirmation messages
- Page titles, meta descriptions and social sharing text
- Accessible labels, image alt text and structured data
- Header, footer and mobile navigation
- Third-party forms or embedded content shown within the site

Use action-led alternatives that match what the customer can actually do:

- Make an enquiry
- Send an enquiry
- Send job details
- Discuss the job
- Arrange an assessment
- Book a measure
- Ask about pricing

An estimate for residential work or written pricing for commercial work may be explained as part of the process. Neither should be presented as a quote or used to create a quote-led call to action.

## 2. Voice and writing rules

The site should sound like a small, experienced local operator. The writing should be practical, calm and specific.

### Use

- First-person wording such as `we`, `our team` and `send us the details`
- Familiar service names customers are likely to search for
- Specific places, equipment, training and response information
- Short paragraphs with one main idea in each
- Active voice and everyday New Zealand English
- Direct action labels such as `Make an enquiry` and `Call for urgent water damage`
- Honest limits when a result depends on the material, stain, weather or property

### Avoid

- Em dashes
- Forced three-part slogans or repeated lists of three
- Contrast formulas such as `it is not X, it is Y`
- Broad claims such as `best`, `unmatched`, `perfect` or `industry-leading`
- Stock phrases such as `bringing spaces back to life`, `your trusted partner` or `tailored solutions`
- Claims that have not been confirmed by the client
- Long introductions that delay the service, location or next step

This approach follows established web-writing guidance: put useful information first, keep it scannable, use active voice and favour clear wording over promotional language.

## 5. Homepage SEO and search experience strategy

### Search intent

The homepage should target people who are actively looking for a local carpet cleaner and want to confirm services, coverage, trust and availability before making contact.

The search journey is usually:

1. Search for a service and location.
2. Confirm that the business covers the property.
3. Check experience, certification, insurance, equipment and reviews.
4. Call or make an enquiry.

The homepage should answer those needs in that order. Search visibility and conversion are treated as one job.

### Primary keyword

> carpet cleaning Central Otago

This is the main homepage search theme. Use the exact wording in the page title, then reflect the same service and location naturally in the H1, opening copy and one later section.

### Supporting homepage keywords

- carpet cleaning Cromwell
- carpet cleaning Southern Lakes
- carpet cleaning Maniototo
- commercial carpet cleaning Central Otago
- pest control Central Otago
- upholstery cleaning Central Otago
- carpet stain removal Central Otago

Major town names should appear naturally in the service area section:

- Cromwell
- Alexandra
- Queenstown
- Wanaka
- Clyde
- Hāwea
- Ranfurly

Do not force every keyword or town into every section. A term should only appear where it helps a customer understand the service or coverage.

### Basis for the keyword set

The target set is based on the business priority, current local search-result language and the service-plus-location pattern used by people looking for nearby providers. It is not presented as a search-volume forecast.

Search Console data should refine the wording after launch. Google Ads Keyword Planner can be used later if the business wants paid-search volume estimates.

### Keyword ownership by page

The homepage should remain focused on the broad local carpet-cleaning intent. Each service page should target its own more specific search terms:

| Page | Main search theme |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | carpet cleaning Central Otago |
| Carpet Cleaning | hot water extraction and professional carpet cleaning |
| Pest Control | pest control Central Otago |
| Upholstery Cleaning | upholstery and couch cleaning Central Otago |
| Stain Treatment | carpet stain removal and pet stain treatment |
| Flood Restoration | 24/7 flood and water damage response |
| Remaining service pages | their service name plus the genuinely served region |

This separation reduces keyword overlap and gives each service page a clear purpose.

### On-page placement

- Put the primary keyword near the start of the page title.
- Use one clear H1 that includes the service and wider region.
- Mention Cromwell and the wider coverage in the first paragraph.
- Use descriptive service links such as `View Carpet Cleaning`.
- Keep service and location wording in normal sentences instead of standalone keyword lists.
- Include the main phone number, hours, emergency availability, certification and insurance as visible text.
- Keep the service area section easy to scan and link to it from the header.

### Local trust and relevance

- Keep the business name, primary phone, email, hours and service areas consistent with the Google Business Profile.
- Maintain one accurate service-area Business Profile rather than creating profiles for towns without staffed locations.
- Add approved real work photos and respond to genuine Google reviews.
- Use the approved customer logos as supporting trust, not as a replacement for service information.
- Link the homepage to every service page and link service pages back to related services and contact options.

### Technical SEO

- Keep the existing canonical URL, sitemap and robots file.
- Use a unique homepage title and description.
- Add an Open Graph image using approved real work photography.
- Set the document language to `en-NZ`.
- Keep the main content in rendered HTML so customers and search engines receive the same information.
- Update the existing `LocalBusiness` structured data with the confirmed main phone, email, area served and service offers.
- Keep IICRC certification and insurance as visible page content instead of inventing unsupported structured-data properties.
- Do not invent or expose a street address. Treat the business as a service-area business unless the client confirms that customers visit a staffed location.
- Add the confirmed opening hours of 9am to 5pm every day to the visible page and `LocalBusiness` structured data.
- Keep the confirmed rating and reviews visible on the page. Do not add self-serving review or aggregate-rating structured data unless current Google eligibility guidance clearly supports it.
- Validate structured data with Google’s Rich Results Test.

### Search experience and performance

- Keep `021 333 354` visible and easy to tap on mobile.
- Place the 24/7 flood call action near the top of the page.
- Show the four priority services before the longer service list.
- State same-day normal enquiry response beside the form.
- Compress the hero video, provide a lightweight poster and avoid delaying the main heading or buttons.
- Reserve image dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images while keeping the hero media lightweight.
- Test Core Web Vitals on mobile and desktop after implementation.

### Practices to avoid

- Do not repeat `carpet cleaning Central Otago` mechanically.
- Do not create near-identical town pages with only the location name changed.
- Do not hide lists of towns or keywords in footers, alt text or structured data.
- Do not write to a target word count. Include the information customers need, then stop.
- Do not use vague headings simply to insert a keyword.
- Do not let SEO wording override the no-quote rule or the natural local voice.

### Measurement after launch

- Connect and verify Google Search Console.
- Submit the XML sitemap.
- Track homepage impressions, clicks, search terms and click-through rate.
- Track taps on the main phone number, urgent flood calls and enquiry-form opens.
- Add campaign parameters to the website link in Google Business Profile so its traffic can be separated.
- Review performance after enough data has accumulated, then adjust titles or copy based on real queries instead of adding speculative keyword variations.

## 11. Research used for the plan

### Local search and SEO

- [Google Business Profile local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091): local results are mainly shaped by relevance, distance and prominence. Complete business information, reviews and accurate details help customers and Google understand the business.
- [Google service-area business guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481): service areas should be accurate, specific and maintained through one eligible profile for the operating location.
- [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide): use clear page titles, useful page structure, descriptive links, relevant images and people-first content. Google does not use the meta-keywords tag and does not recommend keyword repetition or a target word count.
- [Google people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): demonstrate real experience, answer the visitor’s need and avoid content created mainly to attract search traffic.
- [Google title-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link): titles should be descriptive, concise and free of keyword stuffing.
- [Google meta-description guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet): descriptions should be unique, readable and specific to the page rather than keyword lists.
- [Google internal-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable): crawlable links and descriptive anchor text help users and search engines understand related pages.
- [Google LocalBusiness structured-data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business): structured data can describe business details, hours and other verified local information.
- [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies): keyword stuffing and near-identical doorway pages created for similar location searches can harm search visibility.
- [Google page-experience guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience): mobile presentation, secure delivery, Core Web Vitals and an unobstructed main experience all contribute to a useful page.
- [Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals): current Core Web Vitals measure loading, interactivity and visual stability.

### Web writing and human voice

- [GOV.UK content principles](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background): short sections, plain words, specificity and active verbs.
- [GOV.UK clear-language guidance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/): familiar language, short sentences and direct actions.
- [Nielsen Norman Group web-writing guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/be-succinct-writing-for-the-web/): concise, scannable content with meaningful headings and important information first.
- [Mailchimp voice and tone guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/): clarity before entertainment, active voice and tone suited to the reader’s situation.
