The page changes described below are prepared in the local site project and have not been published yet. “Live” in the next section refers to the production site checked before these edits.

## Full page SEO plan

### Search intent and target terms

| Intent | Primary searches | Page answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a local provider | pest control Central Otago, pest control Cromwell, pest control Queenstown, pest control Wanaka, pest control Alexandra | The title, H1, service-area section, confirmed town coverage, contact details and enquiry action. |
| Find help for a pest | spider control, fly control, rat and mouse control, wasp nest removal, flea treatment, bed bug treatment, carpet beetle treatment, silverfish, borer | Individual pest headings, scope of assessment and what the customer should report. |
| Plan a visit | pest treatment preparation, pets during treatment, re-entry, follow-up treatment | The pre-treatment section, process, after-treatment section and visible FAQs. |
| Check fit | pest control for homes, rentals, accommodation and businesses | Property-use information in the service, pest and coverage sections. |

The page should accurately target **Central Otago and the confirmed Southern Lakes service area**. “Otago” alone can imply coverage as far as Dunedin, which is not confirmed. The current FAQ makes that boundary clear.

### Page structure now

1. **H1: Pest control in Central Otago and Queenstown.** Establishes service and geography in the first viewport.
2. **Is pest control right for your property?** Helps visitors recognise whether the service fits their situation.
3. **What our pest control service includes.** Explains assessment, treatment and instructions.
4. **Pests we treat.** Lists the confirmed service scope from the client pest-control PDF.
5. **Different pests need different treatment.** Six substantial subsections for spiders/flies, rats/mice, wasps, fleas/bed bugs, other listed insects, and safety/follow-up.
6. **Before pest treatment.** Gives the customer a practical preparation list without making product-specific safety promises.
7. **What to expect / After pest treatment.** Shows the process and sets follow-up expectations.
8. **Pest control across Central Otago and Southern Lakes.** Names confirmed towns and tells the customer what location details to supply.
9. **Pest Control questions / enquiry.** Answers remaining booking and safety questions, then offers a clear contact action.

The title, H1 and local H2 already place “Central Otago” prominently. The town H3s cover city searches. Adding “Central Otago” to every pest heading would make the page harder to read and risks unnatural repetition. [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) recommends relevant terms in the title and main heading, while its [spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) warns against repetitive city and keyword blocks.

### Larger sections to add when the business can supply proof

| Section | What it should contain | Needed before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recent pest-control work in the region** | Two or three concise real examples: pest, property type, town, what was assessed, work completed and any follow-up. Real work photos would make this the strongest addition. | Technician records, approval to describe the job, and photo/customer consent. Avoid invented outcomes. |
| **Seasonal pest activity** | Technician-approved guidance on when common pests become more noticeable locally, and when to contact the team. Link to the relevant treatment subsection. | The team's actual seasonal experience and approved wording; do not guess a month-by-month calendar. |
| **Pest control for accommodation and rentals** | How the team coordinates access, affected rooms, preparation and guest/tenant use of the space. | Confirmation of the service workflow, any documents supplied and any booking constraints. |
| **Treatment methods and safety** | Which approaches the team actually offers for each pest, what preparation is needed, product-specific re-entry advice and whether follow-up is typical. | Technician review of methods, approved products and safety instructions. Never publish one universal “safe for pets” promise. |

These sections should provide firsthand evidence and answer customer questions. Length on its own is not a ranking target. [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether a visitor learns enough to achieve their goal and whether the page shows firsthand expertise.

### On-page and technical checks

- Keep one canonical, indexable pest service URL. The live URL and sitemap currently meet this requirement.
- Keep the title and description specific, readable and consistent with visible content. Review Search Console click-through data before changing them again.
- Keep Service and LocalBusiness structured data truthful. Breadcrumb structured data now mirrors the visible trail. FAQs are useful on the page, but Google generally does not show FAQ rich results for ordinary business sites.
- Link to the pest page from genuinely relevant site surfaces. The home service card, services index and footer already link to it. Add contextual links only where the surrounding copy actually discusses pest control.
- Keep image descriptions factual. Replace general imagery with approved local job photos when available, with alt text describing the actual scene rather than city keywords.
- Recheck mobile readability, enquiry access and loading after each substantial content addition.

### Measurement and decision points

1. Record current Search Console impressions, clicks, average position and search terms for the pest URL; separate Central Otago, Cromwell, Queenstown, Wanaka and Alexandra queries.
2. After publication and recrawl, compare the same queries and actual enquiries. Search Console position is an average, so also check searches from each town rather than treating one rank as universal.
3. If a town is gaining impressions but not clicks, test the title/description against the query. If a town has no impressions, add real local proof before considering a separate town page.
4. Review the Business Profile alongside site performance. Local pack visibility depends heavily on the profile, proximity, reviews and prominence, not just page copy.

## Next actions outside the codebase

1. **Google Business Profile:** Confirm that the Cromwell business profile is verified, uses the real business name, correct phone, hours and service area, and lists pest control among its actual services. Add current pest-work photos. Avoid a second Queenstown or Wanaka profile without a separately staffed eligible location. [Google's local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says relevance, distance and prominence all matter; its [service-area rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) limit when multiple profiles are allowed.
2. **Reviews and proof:** Ask recent pest-control customers for honest Google reviews and, with consent, gather one real treatment photo or case example from each priority town. Do not invent pest-specific testimonials or before/after outcomes.
3. **Search Console:** Inspect and request indexing of the canonical pest URL, submit the sitemap if needed, and track queries and clicks for `pest control` with Cromwell, Queenstown, Wanaka, Alexandra and Central Otago. Compare results by query and page after Google recrawls. Rankings vary by searcher location and device.
4. **Town pages only with distinct evidence:** If the business can supply real work examples, local photos, customer questions, coverage details and useful advice for each town, create genuinely useful location pages. Do not publish near-identical city swaps; [Google's spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) calls those doorway pages.
5. **Local authority:** Keep the name, phone and service details consistent on relevant trade listings and local business directories. Seek genuine mentions and links from local partners or associations where the business has a real relationship.

Google states that no one can request or pay for a better organic local ranking. The site changes improve relevance and coverage, but a number-one result cannot be promised or made immediate. The quickest owner-controlled work is usually the Business Profile, real reviews and proof, followed by Search Console measurement.

## Source notes

- [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials): helpful content, prominent search terms and crawlable links.
- [Google LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business): represent the real business accurately and validate structured data.
- [Google FAQ appearance changes](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes): FAQ rich results are generally limited to well-known government and health sites. The FAQs here are for visitors and search relevance, not an expected FAQ rich result.
